Why the chain rule actually works

25%
Tutor · 2 min ago

When two changes compose, the rate of the whole is the product of the rates. That sentence is the chain rule in one breath; everything else is unpacking it.

Before we touch any notation, let me ask: have you seen the rule written down before? If yes, how was it introduced?

You · 1 min ago
I've seen (f ∘ g)' = f'(g) · g' but I don't really feel why it's the product. I can apply it on autopilot.
Tutor · just now

That's the most common starting point and we're going to dismantle it. The formula compresses a story about nested rates into eight symbols.

Suppose a balloon is being inflated and you track its radius over time. The radius grows at some rate. But what you actually care about is the volume. If the radius is growing at 2 cm/s when the balloon is 5 cm across, how fast is the volume changing right then?
