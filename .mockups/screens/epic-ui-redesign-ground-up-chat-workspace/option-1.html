Why the chain rule actually works

A short discussion before three worked examples and a quick check.
Tutor · 2 minutes ago

When two changes compose — when the position depends on time but the velocity also varies, when the price depends on supply but supply depends on weather — the rate of the whole is the product of the rates. That sentence is the chain rule in one breath; everything else is unpacking it.

Before we touch any notation, let me ask: have you seen the rule written down before? If yes, I'd like to know how it was introduced; if not, that's actually preferable for what comes next.

You · 1 minute ago

I've seen (f ∘ g)' = f'(g) · g' but I don't really feel why it's the product. I can apply it on autopilot.

Tutor · just now

That's the most common starting point and it's the one we're going to dismantle. The formula compresses a story about nested rates into eight symbols; the symbols become memorable once the story does. Let's start with a concrete case.

Suppose a balloon is being inflated, and you track its radius over time. The radius grows at some rate. But what you actually care about is the volume — and volume depends on radius. If I tell you the radius is growing at 2 centimeters per second when the balloon is 5 centimeters across, how fast is the volume changing right then?

fig. 1 · Symbolic check via Sympy — d/dt of V(r(t))
V(r) = (4/3) π r³  ·  r(t) = 5,   dr/dt = 2
dV/dt = 4 π r² · dr/dt = 4 π (25)(2) = 200 π cm³/s ≈ 628.3
✓ verified · sympy.diff(V.subs(r, r_t), t)
sub-agent · grader · 800ms Your earlier free-response on related rates (lesson 2) used the chain rule implicitly without naming it; that's why the volume-from-radius framing here is the right re-entry point.

Notice what just happened. The volume changed at 200 π cubic centimeters per second — and that number came from two rates multiplied: the rate at which volume changes with respect to radius (the geometry), times the rate at which radius changes with respect to time (the physics of inflation). The chain rule is just this, stated generally.
