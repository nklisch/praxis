¶ Your explanation

Imagine you're blowing up a balloon. As you blow air in, the balloon's radius grows.

But what you really care about isn't the radius — it's how much space the balloon takes up, the volume. The volume gets bigger because the radius gets bigger.

Here's the thing: there are two speeds happening at once. The first speed is how fast you're blowing air in — that's how fast the radius is changing.

The second speed is how much extra volume you get for each tiny bit of extra radius — that depends on how big the balloon already is, because a bigger balloon balloons up faster.

To figure out how fast the volume is growing, you multiply those two speeds together.

Not add them. Not "cancel" them. Multiply. Because two changes are stacked on top of each other.
