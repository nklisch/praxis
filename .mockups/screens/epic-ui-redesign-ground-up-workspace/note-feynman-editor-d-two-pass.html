The chain rule, in plain words

Re-reading your own explanation. Where do you pause and think "I'm not sure why I said that"? Select that text and mark it — the gap appears in the margin next to it. The tutor reads your gaps next session.

Imagine you're blowing up a balloon. As you blow air in, the balloon's radius grows.

But what you really care about isn't the radius — it's how much space the balloon takes up, the volume. The volume gets bigger because the radius gets bigger.

Here's the thing: there are two speeds happening at once. The first speed is how fast you're blowing air in — that's how fast the radius is changing. The second speed is how much extra volume you get for each tiny bit of extra radius — that depends on how big the balloon already is, because a bigger balloon balloons up faster.

To figure out how fast the volume is growing, you multiply those two speeds together. Not add them. Not "cancel" them. Multiply them. Because two changes are stacked on top of each other.

That's the chain rule. Two rates, stacked, multiplied. The formula people write down is just a shorthand for that sentence. But I avoided the word "derivative" entirely — I don't know if my explanation would survive contact with the actual notation.
