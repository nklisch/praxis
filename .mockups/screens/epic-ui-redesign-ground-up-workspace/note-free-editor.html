Started yesterday, 5:42 pm · linked to Calculus

I came back to calculus because the actuarial exam needs it and I've been faking it for two years. The goal isn't passing — it's actually understanding the rates story so I stop guessing on problems with mixed variables.

When I was nineteen, calculus was a wall I climbed by memorization. I got an A in Calc I and a B in Calc II and I remember almost none of it. The formulas were just runes I copied; I could apply them on a quiz but never explain them. And it worked, mostly. That's the part that bothers me now. The system rewarded a shape of understanding that wasn't understanding.

Praxis bothering me to write Cornell summaries and explain things to a 12-year-old is — annoying. It's exactly what I would have hated at nineteen. It's also exactly what I needed and didn't get. I'm forty-one and the second pass at this is going to feel slower because I'm refusing the shortcut.

I don't want to be the kind of actuary who's right because the formulas are right. I want to be the kind who's right because the story is right and the formulas fall out of it. That's what I'm here for. The exam is a forcing function.
