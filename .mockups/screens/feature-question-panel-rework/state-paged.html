When you write (f ∘ g)'(x) = f'(g(x)) · g'(x), why is the argument of f' still g(x), not x?
Because f'(x) and f'(g(x)) are interchangeable
Because f operates on the output of g, so its rate is evaluated at g(x)
Because g(x) is just shorthand for x in this notation
Because the product rule rearranges f' to use g(x)
