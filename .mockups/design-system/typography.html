Locked typography · Option A

System Editorial

Serif-led editorial typography using only system-installed faces. Iowan Old Style on macOS, Sitka Text on Windows, Charter or Source Serif on Linux — all warm humanist serifs with strong italics. Zero remote font fetch: satisfies the Electron CSP without exception, renders correctly the moment the app opens, no .woff2 assets to ship. Cross-OS face variation is accepted as a deliberate trade for the offline guarantee.

Considered and rejected

Option B — Fraunces + Newsreader + JetBrains Mono. Distinctive hosted faces with extraordinarily expressive italics and a SOFT axis on Fraunces. Visually stronger than the system stack, but would require self-hosting .woff2 assets for the Electron CSP and shipping bundled fonts adds startup weight without a clear win for an editorial app whose system-serif baseline is already strong. Revisit if the project ships hosted-only.

Why the chain rule actually works
A short discussion before the worked examples.

When two changes compose — when the position depends on time but the velocity also varies, when the price depends on supply but supply depends on weather — the rate of the whole is the product of the rates. That sentence is the chain rule in one breath; everything else is unpacking it.

Notice what the rule does not say. It does not say to multiply the variables. It does not say derivatives "cancel." It says: when changes nest, their rates multiply. We'll trace that idea through three small examples before we let any formal notation through the door.
