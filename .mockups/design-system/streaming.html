design system · chat streaming

How streaming text should feel

The standardized chat-streaming pattern for every chat-bearing surface in Praxis. .chat-turn__streaming-tail wraps each arriving chunk and fades it in via the chat-fade-in keyframe at --dur-ambient (480ms). The pulsing dot on .chat-turn__streaming is the persistent in-flight signal. No cursor, no caret blink, no terminal-style chrome. Reads as someone thinking and writing, per the assertion in docs/UX.md § "Streaming with intercept and easing".

Locked direction · 2026-05-24

Pace = medium · 120ms / chunk · Chunk = word · Fade = gentle · 480ms opacity (var(--dur-ambient))

Picked from the controls below after A/B exploration. The fade is already the .chat-turn__streaming-tail default in components.css — no further CSS change needed. The 120ms / word-chunk pace is the target for the production streaming hook's ring-buffer release schedule (`packages/ui/src/...` — Phase 13 streaming hook). Controls below remain for future revisits; this is the standing answer until someone explicitly re-opens the question.

Two-element pattern.
1. .chat-turn__streaming on the speaker line — pulsing dot + "streaming" label. This is the persistent signal; it tells the eye "still arriving" without competing with the text. Vanishes the moment the turn settles.
2. .chat-turn__streaming-tail wraps just-arrived text — fades opacity 0 → 1 over --dur-ambient. Each new chunk wraps in a fresh span; the previous chunks have already settled to opacity 1 and stay that way. Settled spans can be unwrapped at any time (the class is structurally neutral).

Edge: long-paragraph streaming stress

When the tutor streams a long paragraph, only the trailing chunk wraps in .chat-turn__streaming-tail. Earlier sentences have already settled at opacity 1. The fade is local to the head of the stream — never re-animates settled text.

Reject list · what we are NOT doing refusals

explicitly refused — 2026-05-24
  • Blinking cursor / caret (▍). Terminal-style chrome; competes with the editorial voice of Studio Quiet; perpetual motion on body text fights the "anti-notification" stance locked in motion.css. The persistent in-flight signal lives on the speaker line, not in the body.
  • Right-edge mask gradient (text fades out at the right edge of each line). Breaks with multi-line wrapping — masks real content, not the head of the stream. The fade-in pattern is per-chunk on append, not per-line on layout.
  • Character-by-character animation of settled text. Once a chunk has rendered, it never re-animates. Re-renders would cause flicker on tab switches, scrolls, and content-window resizes.
  • Word-by-word animation of full settled paragraphs. Reserved for the head of the stream. Animating all text on mount would feel like a Karaoke transition.
  • Spinner / loading-state element in the body. Mode-violation — the speaker-line dot already carries this. A body-mounted spinner reads as "the system is waiting on tutor" rather than "tutor is speaking".
  • Squash, scale-up, or overshoot on chunk arrival. Locked Productive motion attitude rejects Disney principles. Fade-in is opacity only; geometry is constant.

Reduced-motion a11y

Under @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) every chat animation collapses to no-op via the guard in components.css. .chat-turn__streaming-tail renders at opacity 1 immediately on mount (no fade); the speaker-line dot stops pulsing (becomes static). The visual hierarchy and content stay legible — just kinetically muted.

Production translation how this maps to code

The streaming hook in packages/ui/src/:

On each new chunk from the engine event stream, the streaming hook:
1. Removes .chat-turn__streaming-tail from the previous trailing span (it's settled now — class drop is a no-op since the fade-in animation: ... both already left it at opacity 1).
2. Appends a fresh <span class="chat-turn__streaming-tail">{newChunk}</span> at the end of the body.
3. On turn-end event, removes the .chat-turn__streaming badge from the speaker line and unwraps the trailing span (or leaves it — opacity-1 wrapper is visually neutral).

The hook owns the wrap/unwrap; the CSS owns the fade. Neither knows about the other beyond the class name.
