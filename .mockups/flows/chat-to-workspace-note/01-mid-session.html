Why the chain rule actually works

You · 2 min ago
I've seen (f ∘ g)' = f'(g) · g' but I don't really feel why it's the product.
Tutor · 1 min ago

That's the most common starting point. Suppose a balloon is being inflated and you track its radius over time. The radius grows at some rate. But what you care about is the volume.

Tutor · just now

Notice what just happened. The rate of the whole is the product of the rates. Volume changed at 200π cm³/s — and that number came from two rates multiplied: the rate at which volume changes with respect to radius, times the rate at which radius changes with respect to time.

The chain rule is just this, stated generally.
